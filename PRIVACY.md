# Plexiform privacy notice

Plexiform is a desktop app that watches your AI coding sessions and shows their state as a small light. This page says what it reads, what it keeps, and what (very little) ever leaves your computer. Things that are planned but not built yet are labelled **Not yet shipped**.

## In short

- **On by default:** checking GitHub for pull requests and builds through your own GitHub login (only if you have the `gh` tool signed in), and reading your Focus / Do Not Disturb status on this computer.
- **Off by default:** calendar, calendar subscription link, voice questions to Claude, voice questions that use the screen, answering permission prompts from Plexiform, and remote devices.
- **The desktop app:** no advertising trackers or general analytics are added by Plexiform. We do not sell your data. The team and provider features below share the selected content with their stated recipients; the website has separate Cloudflare processing.
- **Only if you join a team board:** your cards, handovers and run progress go to your team's hub. Today that hub is run by Plexiform at `app.plexiform.dev`. When you sign in (with an email code, or with Google or GitHub once the hub runs accounts), your name and email are shared with that hub.
- **Update checks (Plexiform's servers):** the app asks `download.plexiform.dev` whether a newer version exists, and downloads it from there only when you ask. Like any download, this reveals your IP address and your app version.
- **The website waitlist:** if you join the waitlist on `plexiform.dev`, we store the email you give us.
- **Cloudflare** passes all of this traffic through (the hub, the website and downloads) as our processor.
- **Crash reports:** recorded and scrubbed on this computer only. Sending them is not built yet (there is no receiving endpoint); when it is, it will stay off until you opt in.


**Team session sharing.** Sessions started in a signed-in team workspace, and terminal Claude channels you deliberately attach there, are automatically shared with that team to watch and send. Personal sessions and unrelated provider chats stay separate. Owners/admins/members can message; viewers can watch. Teammate messages use the session owner's provider quota and existing permissions. Shared input and replies pass through the hub operator in readable form; no transcript from before sharing is exported. Bounded task, input-needed and child-agent metadata is shared only when reported after the share began; each child retains its own report time. You can stop a share in Overview or disable automatic team sharing in Preferences. Automatic sharing does not connect an unconfigured terminal or approve provider permissions.

**Owner-reviewed team proposals.** <!-- flow:interaction-proposals files=board/hub/interaction-proposals.js,src/team-hub-client.js,src/remote-interaction.js --> When you choose “Ask owners to review”, your exact assignment title and message are saved in readable form on the selected team hub, together with your account identity, the shared session, its generation, decision ID/version, review status, expiry and eventual provider receipt ID/state. The requester and the session owner can read these proposals; the hub operator can access stored text. This is separate from live relayed messages. Pending and reviewed records are retained for up to seven days and purged by the hub’s normal cleanup; account deletion and stopping the share remove them. A removed member loses access immediately. Rejecting or revoking a proposal stops its unused grant, but keeps its audit text until retention expires. An approval names one requester, one exact message and session generation, one decision version, one new-turn action and a deadline of at most one hour. It does not enforce a monetary or token budget. The owner’s computer also holds an in-memory copy of its own approved grants and checks it before sending; restarting the host does not restore ended sessions or executable grants. Approval does not remove broader rights already given by “Watch and send”. The requester must deliberately send the approved message; no background retry follows a crash or an uncertain acknowledgement. A send already admitted may finish after revocation, so consult its receipt before requesting another.

**Collision alerts and handing work to a teammate.** When your team's hub reports that one of your live sessions and another session in the same linked repository are editing the same file, Plexiform shows a notification and marks the session on Sessions and Home. It uses only what the hub already sends: the file path, the other session's record id, title and author name. Nothing extra is sent to the hub to work this out; which sessions are yours is decided on this computer from your local session list. "Hand to teammate" on a board card records on the hub who you handed the work to, your name and an optional note (up to 500 characters, secrets removed); that teammate's session brief lists it until they continue it, and continuing marks it taken.

**Claude terminal channel (explicit opt-in).** <!-- flow:claude-terminal-channel files=src/claude-channel-session.js,src/claude-channel-server.js --> You start Claude Code with a private local MCP configuration and explicitly enable its research-preview channel. A per-terminal capability connects Claude's MCP process to a bounded loopback endpoint on this Mac. Plexiform sends only the selected message and shows Claude's correlated acknowledgement and reply. A transport write alone is unconfirmed delivery. No automatic retry or permission approval is provided. The terminal retains its own permissions; optional team sharing uses the policy above. The private capability expires and is revoked when Plexiform stops; it is never included in session metadata.

## What stays on your machine

- **Widget page and Usage optimiser.** The Widget page shows a still of the floating widget's own window, taken on this computer about once a second while the page is open, and changes the same widget settings as Preferences. The Usage optimiser's tool list shows the waste finder's totals from your Claude Code transcripts on this computer (Plexiform Plus). Neither sends anything anywhere.

- **Plugin review (read-only groundwork).** The verifier checks bundled plugin descriptions and exact local source files. The manifest's `agent-plugins.org` schema identifier is checked locally without contacting that site. Private review plans stay in memory; this stage does not install plugins, change agent settings, contact a provider or save a settings journal.

- **Spend safety and plan limits (local only).** To estimate how close a Pro or Max subscriber is to the plan's 5-hour and weekly limits, Plexiform reads the plan tier fields in `~/.claude.json` (`oauthAccount` billing type, organisation type and rate-limit tier; not your name or email) and the token counts it already reads from `~/.claude/projects`. To spot retry loops it reads the last 256 KB of transcripts changed in the last 15 minutes and compares tool names, inputs and whether they failed, in memory only; nothing from them is stored. Your plan choice, the "Show what this would cost on the API" setting and per-project daily caps are kept in `spend-safety.json` in Plexiform's data folder. Nothing here leaves the computer; alerts are desktop notifications and widget messages.

- **Automatic work-card routing (local lookup).** <!-- flow:work-capture-repository files=src/work-capture.js --> Plexiform reads a reported working folder's local Git origin with a bounded, read-only Git configuration command. It loads its own repository-name normalizer locally; this lookup does not contact GitHub or run project commands. A private file records task identities, their pinned board destinations, remembered repository defaults and whether automatic cards are enabled. Each observed session also gets a local work record in a second private file (`work-records.json`, readable only by you, at most 500, finished ones removed after 7 days): a title taken from your latest request (or the task title the AI reported), the first request as its goal (up to 400 characters), the AI's last reply or reported summary (up to 1,500), the files its tools edited and read (relative to the session's folder; files outside it are left out), the branch, status, times and whether a local handover exists. It is built from the same facts as the session handover (hook reports and, where those are missing or older, the session's own transcript, read as described under session handover documents), with secrets and personal data such as email addresses removed and your home folder shown as `~`. On My board the card shows that title, goal, summary and changed files. Once per card, My board cards made before work records existed get the same title and text, built from that session's handover facts or, when those are gone, its stored handover document; this updates the card in place on the local board and never creates a card or touches team cards. Nothing from the work record leaves this computer unless summary sharing is on for that hub (see automatic work cards below).

Plexiform reads and stores the following information locally. The team-sharing and provider features below can relay selected messages, replies and reported metadata; this is not a promise that all session text stays on this computer:

- Your Claude Code conversation logs (the files in `~/.claude/projects`), read to work out tokens and spend. Only counts and costs are kept, not the text.
- Hook reports from Claude Code (and Cursor, Codex or Gemini if you connected them) say that a prompt was sent or a tool ran and feed the session state below. The token/cost reader does not retain full conversation text. Other enabled features retain local task text, pending input details and selected messages or replies as described below; supported team shares and durable board inboxes relay content to the hub.
- **Queued session follow-ups (only when you choose Queue next turn).** <!-- flow:session-followups files=main.js --> Plexiform keeps up to 16 follow-up messages across accounts in an OS-encrypted file, `session-followups/outbox.bin` inside its app-data folder. The file holds the exact message, its target session/share and generation, account/board scope, creation time, delivery status and the provider receipt ID/state when known; it does not save additional response transcripts. No plaintext fallback is used. On packaged Mac builds, a disposable app process performs native cryptography through private anonymous pipes, so a pending Keychain authorization cannot block the main event loop or Quit. A follow-up can dispatch only while Overview is focused on its original account and board, the original session remains current, the provider supports a new turn and any team share freshly permits sending. A team follow-up uses that existing team hub's normal session-message transport and content handling; this is not a new remote encrypted queue. Cancelling an unsent follow-up removes its saved message text; Discard removes its local record. Unsent or refused messages expire after seven days; their text is cleared through an encrypted save before another dispatch, leaving an expired receipt until you discard it. Sent/uncertain receipts and their original message remain until discarded, with a device-wide bound of 16. Encrypted file writes and the containing-directory rename are synced before a dispatch marker permits any provider effect. Platforms/filesystems that cannot attest directory sync refuse queue writes and dispatch; no less-durable fallback is used. Windows native durable replacement remains a separate acceptance gate, so this queue may remain unavailable there. Restart preserves the saved follow-up but never recreates or reattaches an ended session. If a dispatch was interrupted or its receipt is uncertain, the message is marked unconfirmed and is not resent automatically. A successful save means queued locally; only the provider's receipt means acknowledged. Settings → Privacy → Your data lists this outbox as Queued session follow-ups and can delete it on restart before the app opens any stores. The scrubbed export excludes the ciphertext because it cannot inspect or redact encrypted message contents; it names that exclusion. No password-store key or account token is put in the outbox or sent to a renderer.
- Session state: for each session, the folder it runs in, its state, the current tool name, the model name, task counts, and the labels of agents it started (up to 40 characters of the agent's type or of the description the model wrote). When a turn fails, it also keeps up to 120 characters of the error or of Claude's last reply, which can therefore contain a snippet of Claude's own words.
- **Session handover documents (local).** <!-- flow:session-handover-git files=src/session-handover.js --> For every session Plexiform observes, on every connected agent, it keeps a short "handover" file in its data folder (`handovers/`, readable only by you, newest 200 kept). It is built from the hook reports, not by the AI, and says so. When a listed session has no hook reports, or older ones than its last activity (for example it started before Plexiform was connected), Plexiform fills the file in from that session's own transcript on this computer (Claude Code's `~/.claude/projects` and Codex's `~/.codex/sessions` files, read only: the first 64 KB and at most the last 4 MB, never changed), and for an agent without a readable transcript from its own session list (status, waiting permission or question, last activity); this runs shortly after start, every few minutes for missing or out-of-date files, and when you press Write now on the Sessions page. The file holds the first and latest prompt (up to 400 characters each), the files and commands the agent's tools used (commands up to 200 characters), tool counts, how the last turn ended, the folder and the agent's own resume command (`claude --resume`, `codex resume`), and the output of read-only `git status --short`, `git diff --stat` and `git rev-parse` in the session's folder (run locally with a 3-second limit; no network, no hooks, no project commands). Secrets are removed with the same filters as diagnostics, but the files can still contain what you typed, so treat them like your shell history. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you switch on "Share handover with team" for a repository (off by default); then the scrubbed handover goes to that team's hub as a system-written note on the board card you attached the session to (or that was made from it), at most every 15 minutes and 12 an hour, never for a viewer or a repository that is not linked to that team board. The board page of a team hub can read the local handover of such a card only for a repository you switched sharing on for; the personal board on this computer always can. Your attachments of sessions to cards (Sessions > Attach to card) are saved only on this computer. Sessions in a project you muted are skipped, and their file is deleted.
- **Session timeline and Export replay (local; saved only where you choose).** The Timeline page (Sessions > Timeline, or Open timeline on a board card's Handover tab) shows one session in order: the prompts, files edited, commands run, approvals asked and answered, turn ends with an API-equivalent spend estimate, checkpoint +/- line counts and the final status. It is read on demand from the same local sources as the handover (the session's transcript, hook facts, Plexiform's own state log and "What changed" checkpoints) and is not stored. Export replay writes one HTML file to the place you pick in the save dialog, after showing what it includes; Plexiform uploads and hosts nothing. The file has no scripts, links or outside assets. Paths are made relative to the project; the home folder, your user name, this computer's name, email and IP addresses and anything shaped like a secret are removed. Prompts and the AI's replies are left out unless you tick the box. Once saved, the file is yours to share or delete.
- **Search everything (local memory index).** <!-- flow:memory-index files=src/memory/search.js --> So the Search everything page can find past work, a background worker inside Plexiform (a thread of the app, not a separate program; no network) reads your AI tools' own history on this computer, read-only: Claude Code's `~/.claude/projects` files, Codex's `~/.codex/sessions` and `~/.codex/archived_sessions` files and Hermes's `~/.hermes/state.db`, and, only if you tick "Include Gemini CLI and Cursor (experimental)" on that page, Gemini CLI's `~/.gemini/tmp/*/chats` files and Cursor's own chat database. It keeps a search index in Plexiform's data folder (`memory/index.db`, readable only by you): for each session the tool, its id, folder, repository name and branch as the history recorded them, title and times; for each turn your prompt or the AI's text reply (up to 4,000 characters) and the names of the tools and files it used. Tool output (file contents, command output) is never read. Secrets are removed with the same filters as diagnostics before anything is written, but the index can still hold what you typed, so treat it like your shell history. Your session folders are never opened (the repository name comes from the history itself). Projects you muted are skipped and removed from the index. On the free plan only the last 7 days are indexed and searched; Plus covers your full history. Entries stay until you clear the index (Search everything → Index → Clear the index) or delete the data folder, even after the tool deletes its own history. "Open timeline" on a result opens that session's Session timeline page, read on this computer as that page always is. "Open handover" writes a handover for that session to `memory/handovers/` when Plexiform does not already keep one. Nothing in the index leaves the computer; "Copy handover" puts the handover on your clipboard, and "Hand to Codex/Claude" on Plus sends it to a session Plexiform starts (see the owned Codex and Claude Code sessions below).
- Waiting inputs you answer from the widget, only if you turned on "Answer permission prompts from the widget" (off by default). While Claude Code waits for you, Plexiform briefly holds what it is waiting on, in a request file with the request id, session id, folder, tool name, and a fingerprint (SHA-256) that binds your answer to exactly what was shown. What else the file holds depends on the kind:
  - **Permission:** the full tool input (the whole command, file path, web address or agent instructions) and any "allow for this session" suggestions. A command can contain secrets you typed into it.
  - **Plan:** the plan text Claude wants approved.
  - **Question:** the questions and their options.
  - **Elicitation:** the request an MCP server is making of you, including the form it asks you to fill.
  - **Notification and blocked:** not request files. The notification's title and message, or the reason Claude's classifier denied something, is recorded in the session file and only shown.
  - **Dialog:** a startup or permission dialog read from a terminal pane (see below), shown but never answered by Plexiform.
  The request files are readable only by you (mode 0600, in a folder set to 0700) and are deleted when you answer. If you don't answer, the hook removes the file itself when it gives up (about 55 seconds, or 20 for questions), and any left behind is swept after about 75 seconds while Plexiform is running. The key that proves an answer came from Plexiform is held in memory and never written to disk.
- Your settings, rules, and any face photos you add as cameos.
- Git and CI results fetched with your own `gh` login (see the next section).
- Spend: worked out from your Claude Code conversation logs, in a background thread, on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- Calendar and focus status (see below), and terminal details: to jump to a session's terminal, Plexiform records in the session file the terminal's identifiers (seven environment values, the terminal device, and the folder at session start).
- Voice: your speech is turned into text on your device. Audio is never written to disk and the spoken words are never logged.
- Codex activity, when connected: session and turn identifiers, working folder,
  bounded subagent identifiers, fixed event/tool labels and local receipt times.
  The adapter discards prompts, messages, tool arguments/results and transcript
  paths from hook input. It does not read Codex conversations or provider logins,
  answer permissions or treat a child finishing as its parent finishing.
- Copy diagnostics (Preferences → Health): puts a text report on your clipboard only when you click it: the health check results, the app, Electron and OS versions, and recent startup, warning and error lines from `app.log`. Home paths are replaced by `~`; folder, repo, host and user names are hashed or removed; tokens, keys, email and IP addresses are redacted. It never includes prompts, conversation logs or session contents, and nothing is sent anywhere: you choose where to paste it.

Everything below lives in `~/.claude-traffic-light` on your computer:

- `sessions/`: one small file per live session.
- `owned/`: launch records and claims for sessions Plexiform launched (see below).
- `requests/`: pending permission requests and your answers.
- `config.json`: your settings and rules.
- `stats.json`: per-day and per-project counts, times and costs, keyed by folder name.
- `compaction-stats.json` (only once the compactor has run): for each compaction, the provider name, the time, the token counts before and after, and the compaction's own token cost when the provider reports it. Readable only by you (file mode 600); an unreadable or unknown-version file is kept beside it as `compaction-stats.json.corrupt`. No message text, summaries or session identifiers. At most the last 100 compactions plus running totals.
- `cameos/`, including `cameos/index.json`: photos you add and their names.
- `characters/`: characters you hatch, one folder each, holding the character's art and the choices you made. They are made from templates on your computer and are never uploaded.
- **Sprite packs (local, only if you add one).** <!-- flow:sprite-pack files=src/sprite-pack.js --> If you put a folder in `~/.plexiform/sprites/`, Plexiform reads its `pack.json` and the PNG or SVG frames it names, so the widget can show them instead of the drawn character. Files are read only from inside that folder (no symbolic links, at most 512 KB a file, 64 frames a state and 8 MB a pack); SVGs with scripts, event handlers, embedded pages or links to other files are refused, and nothing in a pack is ever run. The frames are passed to the widget window as images. The name of the pack you pick is saved in `config.json`. Nothing from a pack leaves this computer.
- `git-signals.json`: recent pull request and build events with their titles and links, the ids of events already shown, your GitHub login, branch names, local folder paths and the logins of reviewers. It holds no passwords or tokens.
- `busy-ics-cache.json` (only with a calendar subscription link): event times and the repeat rules, status and free/busy flags of those events, plus a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the link, never the link itself. Meeting titles are kept only if you turned on "show meeting name".
- `away.json`: a recap written after any busy spell, including a Focus one (Focus reading is on by default). It lists session ids, folder paths, tool names, the names of rules that held a notification, and why you were busy: the Focus mode's name, or the meeting's title only if you turned on "show meeting name" (up to 60 characters). It is deleted when you dismiss it or it expires.
- `approval-counts.json`: how often you have approved the same command or folder from the widget, for a "make it a rule?" suggestion. Not written for now: the card is hidden and nothing is counted until rules can answer. It holds keyed fingerprints (HMAC-SHA256) of the tool and command or folder, never the command or path itself, with a count and a time; counts older than 30 days are dropped. File mode 0600.
- `approval-secret.json`: the random key those fingerprints are made with, sealed with the system keychain (Electron safeStorage) where available, otherwise a random salt in a file only you can read (mode 0600). Not created for now, for the same reason.
- `last-hook.json`: only the time of the newest hook event and which agent sent it (such as "claude"), so Preferences → Health can show the last hook event after your sessions end.
- **Crash reports (local).** <!-- flow:crash-capture-local files=src/crash-reports.js --> When the app's main process hits an uncaught error or an unhandled rejection, or one of its windows' page processes dies, Plexiform writes a short report to `crash-reports/reports.jsonl`, readable only by you (mode 0600). A report holds only: a random id, the time, the kind of crash, the app version, OS name and version and processor type, and either the error's type, the first line of its message (at most 300 characters) and its code locations, or a page process's exit reason and exit code. It is scrubbed with the same scrubber as Copy diagnostics before it is written: home paths become `~`, your folder, repo and computer names become short hashes, and your username, tokens, emails and IP addresses are removed. Page addresses, window titles, prompts, conversations and file contents are never put in a report. At most 20 reports and 64 KB are kept, oldest dropped first, and at most 25 are written per run. Preferences → Privacy → Crash reports shows the smaller structural upload under "View what would be sent"; "Export diagnostics" saves the more detailed local reports to a file you choose. Its "Allow sending crash reports" switch is off by default and is stored in `crash-reports/consent.json`. Only new structural reports are sent after renewed consent (see Optional crash reports).
- `app.log` and `app.log.old`: diagnostic log. It can contain project folder names, repo names and pull request numbers, file paths (which include your OS username) and notification titles. It never contains prompt text.
- `token` and `port`: the details of the connection on this computer (see below).
- `bin/buddy-hook` (`bin\buddy-hook.cmd` on Windows): a small launcher that Claude Code's hooks call.
- `window-bounds.json`, `manual-override.json`, and first-run marker files.

Electron (the framework Plexiform is built on) also keeps its own app data in `~/Library/Application Support/Plexiform` on macOS, `%APPDATA%\Plexiform` on Windows and `~/.config/Plexiform` on Linux. That is browser-style housekeeping (cache, window state) plus the team sign-in, not session data. Before the rename that folder was called `claude-buddy`: the first time Plexiform starts it copies that folder across, leaves the original where it was, and leaves out the sign-in, which only the old app can read (you sign in again).

## What changes on your computer

Plexiform changes a few things outside its own folder, and removes them when you uninstall:

- **Hooks.** It adds its hooks to Claude Code's settings file (`~/.claude/settings.json`), and to Cursor's or Gemini's own config if you connect them. Hooks run Plexiform's bundled scripts using its application binary or local hook wrapper. Gemini's Notification hook is read only for its `ToolPermission` type, to show the session as waiting on you; the alert's message and details are not stored. Connecting Codex adds lifecycle hooks to `~/.codex/hooks.json`, preserving other hooks and the separate `config.toml` notification command. Codex requires review and trust of new or changed hook definitions before running them. Plexiform does not change that trust or install Codex activity hooks automatically at startup. Configuration alone is not evidence that a session is active.
- **One permission rule in Claude Code's settings.** The installer adds a single rule under `permissions.deny` in `~/.claude/settings.json`: `Edit(~/.claude-traffic-light/**)`. It stops Claude sessions from editing Plexiform's own data folder, so an agent can't tamper with Plexiform's files. Before the first change, Plexiform keeps a one-time backup of your settings file at `~/.claude/settings.json.buddy-backup`; it is never overwritten. The rule is removed when you uninstall or disconnect Claude Code.
- **AI tools you connect (More → AI tools).** Before it changes a tool's hook file, Plexiform shows the exact lines it will add, and saves a copy beside it as `<file>.plexiform-backup-<time>`; Undo puts that copy back, and Disconnect removes only Plexiform's own entries. A custom tool adds a small script, `~/.local/bin/plexiform-run`, that launches the tool you name and reports only that it started and how it ended (name, folder, exit code) to Plexiform on this computer. Which tools you added and when each was last seen are kept in `ai-tools.json` in Plexiform's data folder.
- **Claude integration (if you turn it on).** One `claude-buddy` entry in `~/.claude.json`.
- **Checkpoints in your git repositories (What changed page, off until you switch it on).** <!-- flow:checkpoint-git files=src/checkpoint-git.js --> At the start and end of each AI turn, Plexiform runs your local `git` in that session's repository to save the project's files (tracked files, and untracked files that are not ignored) as a commit under `refs/plexiform/cp/` in that repository's own `.git` folder. It uses a temporary index, so your staged changes, `HEAD`, branches and working files are not changed. Whole-project restore changes your working files to match a checkpoint, after you confirm, and saves a checkpoint of the current files first. The separate Undo this turn action reviews only that turn’s paths, keeps files changed afterward and saves a safety checkpoint before applying eligible files. It refuses approximate or observed overlapping turns; linked paths, deleted-file recreation, permission changes and Git content transformations are not eligible. Eligible existing files retain their inode, permissions, ACLs and extended metadata. Local turn-attribution metadata is saved with your checkpoint settings, and main-process Undo reviews expire after 45 seconds. Independent programs do not honour Plexiform’s internal lock: stop other writers and review every path before undoing. These git runs never fetch, push or clone (network transports are switched off), never run hooks, filters or external diff programs, and ignore your global and system git config except your global ignore file; a repository whose own config defines filters or includes is skipped. A normal `git push` does not send these refs (only `git push --mirror` would). Folders that are not git repositories are skipped. Untracked files that are not ignored, which can include secrets, end up in the repository's `.git` objects, which git removes on its own garbage collection after the refs are gone.
- **Project safety checks (Home → Project safety).** <!-- flow:guardrails-rotate-link files=src/guardrails-main.js --> After each turn that restore points save, Plexiform scans, on this computer, the lines that turn added and the files it changed for things that look like keys or passwords, for secrets behind names a website copies into the browser (`NEXT_PUBLIC_`, `VITE_`, `EXPO_PUBLIC_`, `PUBLIC_`), and for the Supabase service_role key in browser code. If you say yes for a project, it also checks database rule files (`supabase/migrations/*.sql`, `firestore.rules`, `storage.rules`) for rules that let anyone in. It asks your local `git` which `.env` files are saved in git or not ignored, by name only, and never opens them. Findings are kept in `guardrails/findings.json` (readable only by you) with the project folder, file name, line, the kind of key, a preview with the key itself blanked out, and an id computed from the key with a random value kept in `guardrails/salt`, so the key cannot be read back from it; the AI session id and turn number are kept so "Undo this turn" can find the restore point. No key is stored or shown. While a permission request asks for something that cannot be taken back (`rm -rf`, `git push --force`, `git reset --hard`, `DROP TABLE`, `supabase db reset`, `prisma migrate reset`, a production deploy), the lamp turns red and, when restore points are on, Plexiform saves one in that repository before you answer. Nothing here is sent anywhere. The only network use is "Make a new key": when you click it, your browser opens that provider's key page (one of github.com, platform.openai.com, console.anthropic.com, console.aws.amazon.com, dashboard.stripe.com, supabase.com, console.cloud.google.com, api.slack.com, console.groq.com, console.x.ai); Plexiform sends nothing with it. Delete the `guardrails` folder in Plexiform's data folder to clear it.
- **Policy files (Settings → Privacy and data → Policies), only when you confirm.** Applying a pack writes `.plexiform/policy.json` in the repo you chose, or `policy-default.json` in Plexiform's data folder, after showing you the exact file. Plexiform's Claude Code hooks read it on this computer to refuse tool calls or prompts it does not allow; prompts are checked for secrets and personal numbers on this computer and are never stored or sent. The latest refusal per session is kept in `policy-blocks/` for 7 days, and today's per-repo spend against a cap in `policy-spend.json`.
- **Open at login** (macOS), if you enable it.
- **The first launch after the rename from the old app**, once. Plexiform asks a running copy of the old app to quit (SIGTERM on macOS and Linux, a close request on Windows), and again on later launches while the old app is still installed. It copies the old app data folder, as above; if anything is already in Plexiform's own folder, that is renamed to `Plexiform.pre-migration-<time>` and kept. It points the old app's own entries in Claude Code's settings, Cursor's, Codex's and Gemini's configs, the `claude-buddy` entry in `~/.claude.json` and `bin/buddy-hook` at Plexiform, leaving entries that run anything else alone. Before it changes one of those files it saves a copy beside it as `<file>.pre-plexiform` (never overwritten; a dated copy if one is already there). It turns Open at Login on for Plexiform if the old app had it set up, and says so. It renames `approval-secret.json` to `approval-secret.json.pre-rename`, as the old one is sealed under the old app's Keychain item, and a new one is made. On macOS it then asks whether to move the old app to the Bin, and only does so if you choose Remove.
- **On another machine you pair as a remote device.** Running `node hooks/remote.js pair` there adds Plexiform's reporter hooks (`emit.js --adapter claude`) to that machine's `~/.claude/settings.json`, leaving its other hooks alone, and saves the pairing in its `~/.claude-traffic-light/remote.json` (readable only by you). `node hooks/remote.js unpair` removes both.

### Technical details

For readers who want the exact terms:

- "Fingerprint" is a SHA-256 hash.
- "Readable only by you" is file mode 0600, in a folder with mode 0700.
- "Hooks" are the commands Claude Code runs on events; Plexiform's is `bin/buddy-hook`.
- "Asks GitHub" means `gh api` GET requests to api.github.com. Plexiform keeps the ETags GitHub returns in memory only, not on disk.
- "A connection on this computer only" is an HTTP server bound to 127.0.0.1. The no-token page is `GET /status`. The token is the `x-buddy-token` header, and the `port` file records the port. The optional Claude integration is an MCP server.
- "Calendar subscription link" is an ICS feed (also written webcal://). The cache stores event times plus UID, RRULE, EXDATE, STATUS and TRANSP fields.
- "Terminal device" is the tty.
- Voice questions run `claude -p --model haiku` with `--tools ''`, `--no-session-persistence` and `--max-budget-usd 0.05`.

What the code does today:

- **Stats:** per-day counts and costs are pruned after 60 days.
- **Session files:** swept automatically once they are stale, which is the longer of your "waiting" and "working" stale settings (defaults 4 hours and 6 minutes) plus 12 hours.
- **Crash reports:** the newest 20, within 64 KB; older ones are dropped as new ones are written.
- **`app.log`:** rotated at 512 KB into one `app.log.old` file, which is overwritten at the next rotation. So at most about 1 MB of log is kept.
- **Git events:** at most the last 30 events, and "seen" markers are dropped after 2 days.
- **Permission requests:** removed when answered. A leftover request is swept after about 75 seconds and a leftover answer after 10 minutes, while Plexiform is running.
- **Team hub (if you join):** its change journal is append-only (comment bodies are not copied into it). Presence is held in memory only and expires after 90 seconds. The hub host keeps nightly database snapshots, the last 14. Until accounts ship, deletion is on request; when they ship, a code-confirmed delete anonymises your user and revokes every device and session.
- **Busy cache and `away.json`:** `away.json` is deleted when dismissed or expired; the feed cache is overwritten on each fetch.
- **Backups:** 30 days, and no more than about 200 MB in total, oldest first, but the 5 newest are always kept. A backup saved just before a restore is kept for at least 7 days.
- **Checkpoints:** the last 3 turns per session on the free plan (every turn on Plus), never older than 7 days, plus the 5 newest safety checkpoints saved before restores. Older refs are deleted after each turn and once a day. To remove them all from a repository yourself, run `git for-each-ref --format='delete %(refname)' refs/plexiform/cp/ | git update-ref --stdin` in it.
- **Team activity log (if a repo shares summaries):** the hub keeps activity events 30 days; the latest record of a session that ended goes 7 days after its last update, any other after 30 days. Deleting your account deletes every record you published; deleting a team deletes its records at the next hourly sweep.
- **Sync (if you turn it on):** the encrypted copy on your team hub is kept while your plan is active; 30 days after it ends it is deleted from the hub and its storage (see Encrypted sync above). Removing a computer from sync deletes its key copy at once. Your local sync keys and state (`sync/`) stay until you delete them.
- **Search index (`memory/index.db`):** on the free plan, turns older than 7 days are removed at each update; on Plus it keeps everything until you clear it (Search everything → Index → Clear the index).
- **Everything else** (config, cameos, window position) stays until you delete it.
