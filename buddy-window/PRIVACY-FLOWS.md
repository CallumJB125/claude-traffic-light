# Privacy flows for the Plexiform window (to merge into PRIVACY.md)

These sections use the `<!-- flow:<slug> files=… -->` form that the privacy guard expects. They cover the three flows this branch adds. `local-board-hub` and `open-link-in-browser` are documented elsewhere.

<!-- flow:team-hub-account files=buddy-window/accounts.js,buddy-window/account-flow.js,buddy-window/index.js,buddy-window/oauth.js -->
Before showing the sign-in screen, the app asks the hub which sign-in methods it offers (Google, GitHub, an email code). No personal data is sent. When you choose "Continue with Google" or "Continue with GitHub", the app opens a listener on this Mac that only accepts connections from this Mac (127.0.0.1). It then sends the hub the provider name, this Mac's name and platform, the listener's address, a random check value and a one-way hash of a secret that stays in the app. The hub returns a sign-in page address. The app opens it in your own browser, but only if it is an https page on a public server. After you sign in there, your browser comes back to the listener with a one-time code. The app accepts exactly one matching answer, then closes the listener, or closes it after 5 minutes or when you press Cancel. It sends the hub the code, the check value and the secret, and the hub returns the same device token as an email sign-in. The app never logs the code, the secret or the token. The hub, not this app, talks to Google or GitHub.

When you sign in to a team hub with an email code, the app sends that hub your email address plus a name and platform for this Mac, such as "Jo’s MacBook Pro" and "darwin-arm64". The hub puts these in the email with your 6-digit code, so you can see which device is asking. The code you type goes back to the same hub, and the hub returns a device token. The app seals that token with the macOS Keychain (safeStorage) and sends it only to that hub's exact address. It goes on the app's own requests and, as a header, on the board page's requests. The page itself never sees the token. After that the app asks the hub for your account, your teams, their members and pending invites. It also sends the team, invite, role and account-deletion requests you make. When an invite link names a hub you haven't used before, the app asks you before it contacts that hub at all. A link that points to a private or local network address is ignored. Signing out, or the hub revoking you, deletes the token and the hub's cookies, storage and cache from this Mac.

<!-- flow:team-hub-runner files=buddy-window/device.js,buddy-window/account-flow.js,buddy-window/index.js -->
When you turn on "Run cards" for a team, the app enrols this Mac with that team's hub using your account. It then starts the board runner as a separate process and gives it the hub address, the team and your device token over a private channel, never on the command line or in the environment. The runner doesn't save the token. The runner connects to that hub and runs the cards the team hands it, on this Mac. If you turn on "Share my live sessions", every team on that hub sees the folder name (not the full path) of each Claude session, which agent it is and whether it is working. That setting is off until you turn it on. Signing out stops the runner and deletes its sealed enrolment. When you're still signed in, it also asks the hub to remove this Mac as a runner.

<!-- flow:integration-connect files=buddy-window/index.js,buddy-window/pages.js -->
When you connect an integration (GitHub, Slack, …) from a team's Integrations page, the provider's sign-in opens in a separate app window. That window keeps its own storage for each hub, never the board's, so your hub token is never sent to the provider and the provider's cookies stay out of the board. The window only opens https addresses, and only from a team hub you're signed in to. Its title always shows the site you're on. When the provider sends you back to the hub's callback page, the window closes. Signing out of that hub clears the window's cookies and storage.
