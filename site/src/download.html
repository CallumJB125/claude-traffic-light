Opening it the first time

The published beta builds are unsigned. Your system may ask for confirmation when first opening a build and again after an update. Check the download source and checksum before granting access.

On a Mac

  1. Open the download and drag {{brand.name}} into Applications.
  2. Open it. macOS says it can't check the app for malware. Choose Done.
  3. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security. Scroll to Security and choose Open Anyway next to {{brand.name}}, then enter your password.
  4. Open {{brand.name}} again and choose Open. Updates may require another confirmation.
  5. If macOS asks to use the keychain, verify that the request is from the build you intended to open before granting access. Unsigned updates may ask again; do not approve an unexpected request.
  6. {{brand.name}} adds its hooks to Claude Code's settings the first time it runs, appending only its own entries, and turns on Open at Login (switch it off from the tray menu and it stays off). Restart any Claude Code sessions that were already running.

On macOS 14 and earlier, right-click the app and choose Open instead. If macOS says the app "is damaged" or reports a signature problem, stop, verify the checksum and re-download from the published release. Contact support if it persists; do not bypass that warning.

Windows

Windows downloads are unavailable while native runner and privacy acceptance are pending. These Mac and Linux instructions do not establish Windows support.

On Linux

  1. Make the AppImage executable (chmod +x) and run it. On a recent Ubuntu it also needs libfuse2. Or install the .deb with your package manager.

Got an invite link?

  1. Install and open {{brand.name}} using the steps above.
  2. Sign in using a method offered by your team hub when the app asks. Account and invite acceptance for the prepared candidate is still pending.
  3. Click your invite link again. It opens the app and puts you on your team.

Check your download

Each build is listed with a SHA-512 checksum. To compare it on a Mac or Linux, run this and check it matches:

openssl dgst -sha512 -binary <file> | base64
